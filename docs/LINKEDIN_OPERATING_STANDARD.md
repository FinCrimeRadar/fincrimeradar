# FinCrimeRadar LinkedIn Operating Standard

## Purpose and authority

This standard governs FinCrimeRadar LinkedIn drafting, audits, and distribution recommendations.

Installed LinkedIn skills and plugins are heuristic and drafting engines. They may support hook analysis, human-writing checks, formatting, and draft generation. They are not the final authority on distribution decisions, regulatory accuracy, practitioner credibility, or permission to act.

Apply this order of authority:

1. The user's explicit instruction.
2. `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, and other applicable canonical repository standards.
3. This LinkedIn operating standard.
4. Third-party LinkedIn skill and plugin heuristics.

When this standard conflicts with a LinkedIn skill or plugin heuristic, this standard wins. Preserve the useful parts of the heuristic that do not conflict.

## Audience and objective

Optimise FinCrimeRadar LinkedIn content for credibility and usefulness with financial crime practitioners, not generic virality.

Primary audiences are:

1. AML professionals.
2. Fraud professionals.
3. Sanctions practitioners.
4. MLROs.
5. Financial crime investigators.
6. FinTech compliance professionals.
7. Risk leaders.

Before drafting or auditing, identify one primary objective. Typical objectives are reach, discussion, authority building, follower growth, website traffic, conversion, or registration.

Do not allow a generic engagement heuristic to displace the stated objective.

## Distribution and links

An external link in a LinkedIn post body is not an automatic audit failure.

For reach, discussion, authority building, or follower growth, prefer the external link in the first comment. State that this reduces friction in the post while adding a second manual action and making the destination less immediate.

For website traffic, conversion, registration, or another explicit click objective, a body link is permitted. State that the direct route supports the click objective but may reduce native-platform distribution.

When recommending a first-comment link, provide both:

1. The exact LinkedIn post, without the link.
2. The exact first comment, including the verified relevant URL.

Do not invent or guess a URL.

## Editorial standard

Prefer one substantive idea per post.

If two launches or materially different subjects compete for attention, recommend separate posts.

Where one exists, lead with the strongest practitioner insight, investigation finding, regulatory tension, operational problem, or unexpected conclusion. Do not default to product-launch language.

Use specific practitioner language. Prefer operational consequences, evidence requirements, decision points, control weaknesses, escalation questions, and genuine regulatory nuance over generic creator language.

Avoid:

1. Manufactured vulnerability.
2. Artificial controversy.
3. Engagement bait.
4. Exaggerated viral hooks.
5. Generic creator formulas that weaken professional credibility.
6. Unsupported certainty.

Use British English.

Never use em dash or en dash characters.

Prefer two or three highly relevant hashtags. Do not optimise for hashtag quantity.

## Credibility and source verification

FinCrimeRadar references must remain precise.

For regulatory, legal, enforcement, or quantitative claims, independently verify the underlying primary source where practical. Apply the sourcing, regulatory accuracy, and correction disciplines in `CLAUDE.md`.

Distinguish clearly between:

1. What a primary source establishes.
2. FinCrimeRadar's analysis or application.
3. FinCrimeRadar's operational recommendation.

Do not treat a LinkedIn skill heuristic, generated draft, secondary summary, or model assertion as evidence.

If practical primary-source verification is not possible, identify the unverified claim and qualify, remove, or flag it. Do not silently convert uncertainty into a confident social-media claim.

## Human writing assessment

Use LinkedIn human-writing tools proportionately. Preserve valid specialist terminology, natural sentence variation, and the author's actual judgement.

Do not rewrite sound practitioner language merely to satisfy a generic AI-detection pattern. Do not promise that content will pass an AI detector.

Flag genuine problems such as model leakage, mechanical transitions, stacked formulas, repetitive sentence structures, vague claims, artificial sincerity, and generic conclusions.

## Manual control boundary

LinkedIn activity is manual unless the user explicitly authorises a specific action.

Do not publish, schedule, comment, react, message, connect, follow, edit a profile, or otherwise modify LinkedIn without explicit user authorisation.

Drafting, auditing, research, and recommendations do not constitute authorisation to act.

## Required audit output

When auditing a FinCrimeRadar LinkedIn post, return these sections in this order:

1. Verdict.
2. Primary objective.
3. Hook.
4. Practitioner value.
5. Credibility.
6. Human writing assessment.
7. Distribution assessment.
8. Regulatory accuracy concerns.
9. Recommended changes.
10. Final revised post.
11. First comment, if required.

The verdict must reflect the post's objective and material risks. Do not fail a post solely because it contains an external link.

The final revised post must preserve verified meaning, avoid invented facts, and follow this standard. If a first comment is recommended, include its exact wording and verified URL.
