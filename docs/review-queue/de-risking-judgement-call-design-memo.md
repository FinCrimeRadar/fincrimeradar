# The De-Risking Judgement Call: design memo (Task 2)

Companion to `de-risking-judgement-call-source-pack.md`. Scope: confirm the Intelligence Core, the stage sequence and two scenarios, and propose only the Framework primitives this subject justifies. No prose drafted.

## 1. Intelligence Core

| Element | Answer for this guide |
|---|---|
| Intelligence question | When a UK bank faces a higher-risk correspondent bank or customer relationship, at what point does exit stop being a defensible AML decision and become de-risking, and can the analyst evidence that line to a supervisor? |
| Evidence state | Established: MLR regs 18, 19, 28, 31, 33 as amended, 34; PSRs regs 51B, 51C; PRIN 2A text. Established as non-binding guidance: FATF Guidance, FCA de-risking page, FCTR 12.3. Industry position: JMLSG, carrying the SI 2026/621 update caveat. FinCrimeRadar assessment: residual risk, the outcome ladder, the treatment of the Increased Monitoring list as bearing on the firm's assessment through 33(6)(c), 33(1)(a), 33(1)(g) and 33(7), Consumer Duty on exit. Provisional: JMLSG revisions awaiting HMT approval. Unknown: JMLSG conformity date. HM Treasury's stated objective for the 33(1)(b) narrowing is known from its Explanatory Memorandum para 5.2 (not part of the instrument). |
| Decision object | One relationship outcome from five: ordinary continuation, enhanced continuation, conditional or restricted continuation, discretionary decline or exit, mandatory decline or termination. Plus the Decision Record that defends it. |
| Uncertainty | JMLSG has not conformed to SI 2026/621. FATF lists change each plenary. Whether regulation 31 reaches inability to complete EDD (source pack A4). | Change condition | Named facts that change the duties or the decision path: jurisdiction moves onto the Call for Action list (33(1)(b) and 33(3A) engage), CDD becomes impossible under reg 28 (reg 31 then applies on its own words), a shell bank link surfaces (reg 34(2)), suspicion arises (51C(c) only if its reasonable-grounds and payment-service-nexus conditions are met, and the POCA route), mitigants stop being verifiable. |

Nothing above needs a dedicated visual component. Each element is answered in prose, the Decision Record, the change-condition analysis and the evidence-state labels.

## 2. Stage sequence

The backlog architecture lists nine steps. Proposed sequence, eight stages plus the Decision Record. Two changes to the backlog order, both for defensibility.

1. **Risk identification.** Customer, geography, product, channel, transaction. Regs 18(2), 28(13), 33(6).
2. **Legal constraint check.** Placed second, before any judgement, because it can end the analysis: reg 31 (inability to complete CDD: mandatory termination of an existing relationship under 31(1)(c), and non-establishment at onboarding under 31(1)(b)), reg 34(2) (shell bank), reg 33(1)(b) (Call for Action, EDD content fixed by 33(3A)). This is the only stage whose output can mandate the keep, restrict or exit decision. Other stages carry mandatory duties of their own, for example the 51B notice and reasons at communication (stage 8). Limit: whether regulation 31 is engaged by inability to complete EDD (as opposed to the regulation 28 measures) is unresolved on the text. The legal gate teaches the plain regulation 28 case only until external review returns.
3. **Mitigants and their effectiveness.** Merges the backlog's "available mitigants" and "control effectiveness". FinCrimeRadar assessment: a mitigant that cannot be shown to work should not be counted as one when reaching the outcome. FATF Guidance para 142 (non-binding guidance) and FCTR 12.3.7G (FCA guidance).
4. **Operational capability.** Kept separate. The question is different: can this firm actually run the mitigant at the required intensity (monitoring, staff, data access). FinCrimeRadar assessment: a mitigant that works in principle but exceeds the firm's capacity is a practical input to the decision, and it must not be presented as a lawful ground for refusing or exiting a relationship. FCTR 12.3.3G (FCA guidance) lists MLROs who are "too stretched or under resourced to carry out their function appropriately" as poor practice, and FCTR 12.3.7G lists "Failing to follow up on outstanding information that has been requested during the CDD process" as poor practice. The under-resourcing example is in 12.3.3G, not 12.3.7G. FCA page: exit is "ultimately a commercial one", but FCTR 12.3.8G (FCA guidance) lists over-weighting reputational or business issues in exit decisions as poor practice.
5. **Residual risk assessment.** Labelled FinCrimeRadar assessment, not regulator terminology.
6. **Escalation and approval.** Senior management approval is statutory at 33(3A)(e) and 34(1)(d) for those paths, and FCTR 12.3.8G (FCA guidance, a good-practice example: involving senior management and AML staff in deciding whether to maintain or exit high-risk relationships; it does not specify termination or require exit).
7. **Relationship outcome.** The five-outcome ladder.
8. **Exit execution (conditional).** Only reached if the outcome is discretionary or mandatory exit. Overlays: PSRs 51B and 51C (vintage and user-type conditional), PARs 2015 reg 26, PRIN 2A.2.10G(3) and (5)(iv) only where the Duty applies (section 4A), tipping-off, SAR consent for repayment (reg 31(2)).
9. **Decision Record.** The documented rationale as an artefact, not a stage: facts, assumptions, indicators, mitigants, decision, rationale.

Stage 8 is the only stage that is skipped for some paths, so the sequence is not a straight line. This matters for the recurrence test: Framework's "sequential decision stages" primitive holds, but this subject needs a conditional final stage that APP scam did not.

## 3. Two materially distinct scenarios

Materially distinct in facts, decision context, risk mechanism and the practitioner judgement tested, per the Worked decisions standard.

**Scenario 1: correspondent respondent in an Increased Monitoring jurisdiction (the hard test).**
- Context: institution-to-institution. Respondent bank established in a jurisdiction on the FATF Increased Monitoring list, not on the Call for Action list. Nested access to downstream customers. Some information requests answered, some not.
- Risk mechanism: geography plus opacity of downstream customers.
- Judgement tested: the post 30 June 2026 structure. The Increased Monitoring listing removes one automatic trigger (reg 33(1)(b) no longer applies by listing alone). That is its whole legal effect on 33(1)(b): reg 33(1)(f), the 33(6) risk-factor cases (including 33(1)(a) and 33(1)(g)) and reg 34 continue to operate on their own terms. Reg 34(1) measures still apply for a third-country respondent, and the listing may be weighed under 33(6)(c), 33(1)(a), 33(1)(g) and 33(7), which is a FinCrimeRadar application. The listing does not fix the outcome. Conditional or restricted continuation (for example limited payment types, enhanced monitoring, senior sign-off) is one result an assessment might reach, and so is exit.
- Counterfactual: the jurisdiction moves onto the Call for Action list. 33(1)(b) and 33(3A) now mandate EDD content and senior approval. A Call for Action listing requires EDD under 33(1)(b), with the 33(3A) measures. Reg 33 does not itself mandate exit. This isolates which fact drives the outcome. Use an EDD-only Call for Action jurisdiction for the counterfactual (the FATF page's second tier, Myanmar as at 19 June 2026), not DPRK or Iran, because for those two the FATF calls for countermeasures that include correspondent-relationship measures (source pack B3). Date-stamp it, since the FATF said it would consider countermeasures for Myanmar in October 2026.
- Overlay stage: PSRs 51B may not apply (a bank respondent is ordinarily not a consumer, a micro-enterprise or a charity with income below £1 million, but micro-enterprise and charity status turn on size and income facts, so this is a fact check and not automatic; if it is met, regulation 40(7) disapplication by agreement is possible if the parties have agreed it, and it is not established that the arrangement is a payment services framework contract; the £1 million income limit applies to charities only). PRIN 2A is not shown to apply (a bank respondent is not shown to be within "retail market business", and Principles 6 and 7 may apply instead; source pack C3). Stage 8 is contract notice, documentation and any SAR consent point. Do not assert the 51B position either way in the scenario feedback.
- The respondent's failure to answer information requests tests whether mitigants are verifiable (stage 3). Missing regulation 34 information is also a potential regulation 31 event under the wide reading (source pack A4): escalate to legal and the MLRO, treat it as a potential regulation 31 event, and document the rationale (JMLSG Part I 4.77, Industry position). The scenario does not adopt either reading.

**Scenario 2: a UK small remittance or charity customer, two branches (mandatory versus discretionary).**
- Context: retail or small business, account contract entered into on or after 28 April 2026. Customer is in a segment the FCA page names as often de-risked (money transmitters, charities).
- Risk mechanism: customer-level, not geography. The scenario forks on one fact: is CDD complete?
- **Branch A, CDD cannot be completed.** Beneficial ownership evidence stays incomplete after the firm has tried alternatives (JMLSG 5.2.7: is it a document problem?). Regulation 31(1) applies on its own words where the regulation 28 measures cannot be applied. Whether it also applies where only EDD cannot be completed is unresolved on the text (source pack A4), so Branch A is built only on the plain regulation 28 case, for example beneficial ownership under 28(4) that cannot be identified and verified. Exit overlay: PSRs 51C(a) removes the 51B(1) notice duty.
- **Branch B, discretionary.** CDD is complete. Residual risk stays high after mitigants. There is no suspicion, so 51C(c) does not apply, and regulation 31 does not apply. The analyst has to decide whether exit is defensible, with FATF Guidance para 142 (non-binding FATF guidance on a range of mitigating options before rejection; R.10 (inability to complete CDD) and R.13 (shell banks) are separate FATF bases), the FCA page ("relatively few cases where it is necessary to decline ... solely because of anti-money laundering requirements", "ultimately a commercial one") and FCTR 12.3.8G (FCA guidance: over-weighting reputational or business issues is poor practice) as inputs. Outcome options: enhanced or conditional continuation, or discretionary exit. If exit is chosen, PSRs 51B applies only where all of these are met: an in-scope indefinite-term framework contract entered into on or after 28 April 2026, Part 6 applying to the user (not disapplied under reg 40(7)), and no 51C or 51D exemption engaged. Then 90 days' notice and sufficiently detailed and specific reasons are required (51A gives two months for contracts entered into earlier). Where the reasons would be unlawful to give, PARs 2015 reg 26(9) has an express "unless providing that information would be unlawful" carve-out. PSRs 51B(2)(a) has no such words, and 51B(4) says another legal requirement prevails on conflict. Whether that resolves the tipping-off question in practice is a REVIEW point and the scenario must present it as open, not as an answer.
- Counterfactuals (one per branch, not one per scenario): Branch A, the customer supplies the missing ownership evidence. The inability condition may end. If it ends before a prohibition or termination duty under regulation 31 has crystallised, the trigger is removed prospectively. The text does not say what happens to a duty that has already accrued. On that footing regulation 31 mandates no particular outcome. The firm reassesses on the completed CDD, and the result (ordinary or enhanced continuation, conditional continuation, or a discretionary exit) is not fixed by the regulation. Branch B, a suspicion arises (for example, unexplained third-party funding). 51C(c) applies only if its reasonable-grounds and payment-service-nexus conditions are met (reasonable grounds to suspect that a payment service provided under the framework contract has been used, is being used or will be used in connection with serious crime). Where it applies, 51B(1) does not apply, which removes the 51B notice protections for the user, and 51C(c) neither requires nor authorises exit by itself. The SAR and consent route is separate.
- Overlay stage: live in both branches, with different content. Scope conditions are in section 4A.

These differ on entity type, decision trigger, statutory constraint that binds, and how live the exit overlay is. Neither is a re-skin of the other.

## 4A. Scoping decisions for the exit overlay (applied)

**PSRs 2017 regs 51A to 51D (inserted by SI 2025/688).**
1. Vintage: 51B applies only to indefinite-term framework contracts entered into on or after 28 April 2026. Earlier contracts: 51A, two months' notice if the contract so provides.
2. User type: regulation 40(7) lets Part 6 be disapplied only where the user is not a consumer, not a micro-enterprise and not a charity and the parties have agreed that any or all of Part 6 does not apply ("charity" here means income below £1 million; micro-enterprise status is fixed at the date the contract is entered into). A micro-enterprise or small charity therefore cannot be disapplied. For a bank respondent the position depends on the size and income facts that decide micro-enterprise and charity status, on contract terms and on whether the arrangement is a payment services framework contract at all (not established).
3. What 51C covers: inability to apply CDD as required by regulation 28 (51C(a)), reasonable grounds to suspect that a payment service under the framework contract has been, is being or will be used in connection with serious crime (51C(c), applying only if both conditions are met, and neither requiring nor authorising exit by itself) and the other listed grounds. It does not list a discretionary risk exit.
4. 51B(4): other legal requirements prevail on conflict.
5. Presentation: the guide describes 51B and 51C as a narrow exit-execution overlay on the AML determination, never as part of the AML determination.

**PRIN 2A (Consumer Duty).**
1. Scope: the Duty applies to a firm's "retail market business" (PRIN 2A.1.3G, PRIN 3.2.6R), with "retail customer" defined by activity in the Glossary, and PRIN 3.2.8R limits it to activities within the retail protection rules of the listed sourcebooks. A bank respondent is not shown to be in scope on the provisions read, and Principles 6 and 7 may apply instead (PRIN 3.2.10R, 3.2.11G). FG22/5 describes payment and deposit scope as consumers, micro-enterprises and small charities (income below £1 million). Say "not shown to be in scope", never "never applies" (source pack C3).
2. Text: 2A.2.8R (avoid causing foreseeable harm), 2A.2.10G(3) (identify harm that might arise if a product is withdrawn) and (5)(iv) (allow time and support to find alternatives where a product is withdrawn).
3. Status of the application: PRIN 2A does not mention AML exits. That it applies to an AML account closure is a FinCrimeRadar assessment (Application), labelled as such and flagged for external review. It is not stated as the FCA's position.
4. PRIN 2A.2.13G limits the duty: it "does not mean a firm has a responsibility to prevent all harm".
5. Presentation: overlay only, in stage 8. Application turns on the retail market business and distribution-chain rules (PRIN 3.2.6R to 3.2.8R, including PRIN 3.2.7R: in a distribution chain the Duty applies only to the extent that the firm is responsible for determining or materially influencing retail customer outcomes), and PRIN 2A stays out of scenario verdicts.

## 4B. Actions by reading, and where no single action is safe (FinCrimeRadar recommendation)

Status: FinCrimeRadar recommendation. It is not a regulator's position and not legal advice, and it is subject to the section 7 external review. It feeds Stage 8 and the Red Team Questions. It is not guide prose. For each open point the entry states what each reading requires, what both readings agree on, and where they diverge so that the firm must decide with legal advice and record why. "Holds under every reading" is used only for reading-neutral steps (dated records and escalation records) and for the 51C(c) limb of entry 3.

1. **Reg 31 and an EDD-only failure.** Scope: the reg 28 measures have been applied, but EDD information is missing. Which EDD is mandatory depends on the case. In a 33(1)(b) (Call for Action) case, 33(3A)(a) to (f) must be included, and source of funds and source of wealth at 33(3A)(c) is confined to that case. In a 33(1)(f) case, 33(4) requires examining the background and purpose and increasing monitoring. For a third-country payments correspondent, 34(1)(a) to (f) apply. In other 33(1) cases the content is a firm judgement under 33(5) ("may also include") and 33(6) to (7).
   - Narrow reading requires: reg 31 is not engaged by an EDD failure alone. Continuing is permitted only if no mandatory reg 33 item remains unmet. If a mandatory item is still unmet (for example 33(3A)(c) in a 33(1)(b) case), the firm is not applying EDD as reg 33 requires, and continuing is not safe on this reading either.
   - Wide reading requires: inability to complete EDD is inability to apply "customer due diligence measures", so reg 31(1)(a) to (c) apply. The firm must not carry out transactions through a bank account, must not establish a relationship, and must terminate an existing relationship, subject to the reg 31(2) repayment proviso and the 31(3) to (5) exceptions. The interim steps below do not suspend 31(1)(a) to (c) under this reading.
   - Both readings agree: mandatory EDD items cannot be ignored, and a POCA or Terrorism Act disclosure must be considered on the facts.
   - Holds under every reading (reading-neutral steps): escalate at once to the MLRO and senior management with a dated record of what was requested, when and the reply, and record which reading the firm adopts and why.
   - Where the readings diverge, the firm must decide with legal advice and record why: whether service can continue at all. Interim measures (no new products, no limit increases, enhanced monitoring of existing activity) are compatible with a later mandatory termination only if the firm actually terminates once it adopts the wide reading. Continued service beyond the date on which legal advice is obtained is itself the risk, so the interim period must be short and dated.

2. **Reg 33(1)(c) reach.** Situation: a correspondent-type relationship with a UK institution, or one that does not involve the execution of payments (for example securities settlement). Reg 33(1) requires "enhanced customer due diligence measures and enhanced ongoing monitoring". Its extent for a 33(1)(c) case is not fixed by 33(3), which only defines terms for 33(1)(b). It is shaped by 33(5) ("may also include"), by 33(6) to (7), and by reg 34 where 34 applies.
   - Signpost reading requires: for a relationship within the reg 34(1) scope (third-country respondent, execution of payments), the reg 34(1) measures. For other relationships within the reg 34(4) definition, no EDD duty arises from 33(1)(c) itself, although 33(1)(a) and 33(1)(g) require EDD where the firm assesses the relationship as higher risk.
   - Independent-trigger reading requires: EDD and enhanced ongoing monitoring for every relationship within the reg 34(4) definition, with the reg 34(1)(a) to (f) content as a template and the extent set by 33(5) to (7).
   - Both readings agree: relationships within the reg 34(1) scope need the reg 34(1) measures, and higher-risk relationships outside it need EDD under 33(1)(a) or 33(1)(g).
   - Where the readings diverge, the firm must decide with legal advice and record why: whether a low-risk relationship outside the reg 34(1) scope needs EDD. Applying actual EDD and enhanced ongoing monitoring to it is lawful under both readings but may be disproportionate (FATF INR.1 paras 13 to 15 expect proportionate measures), so the record of the risk reasoning is what defends the choice.
   - Senior management: for an existing relationship, senior management sign-off of the EDD outcome is recommended governance, supported by FCTR 12.3.7G (FCA guidance), whose good-practice example reads "Senior management/senior committee sign-off for new correspondent banking relationships and reviews of existing ones". It is not statutory. Establishment approval applies only to a new relationship: reg 34(1)(d) says "before establishing a new correspondent relationship". A broad reading of 33(1)(c) does not itself import 33(3A)(e), which is confined to the 33(1)(b) case.

3. **Criminal prohibition versus 51B(4).** Situation: the firm has made a disclosure, and a 51B notice with reasons would otherwise be due.
   - Reading A requires: a tipping-off offence is a "legal requirement", so 51B(4) displaces the 51B(1) to (3) reasons duty "to the extent of the conflict". Reading B requires: it is not, so the 51B reasons duty stands in full.
   - Both readings agree on the offence elements: POCA s333A(1) and TA s21D(1) require a disclosure of a matter within subsection (2) (that a disclosure has been made), likely prejudice to an investigation, and regulated-sector provenance. Subsection (3) covers disclosing that an investigation is contemplated or carried out. POCA s333D(3) and TA s21G(3) give no offence where the person does not know or suspect that the disclosure is likely to have the prejudicial effect. Explaining the underlying conduct without revealing a report or an investigation may therefore be possible, and is fact specific.
   - Holds under every reading where 51C(c) applies (both its reasonable-grounds and payment-service-nexus conditions are met): 51B(1) does not apply, so no 51B notice is due. Record why 51C(c) is met. This does not mean that no notice at all is due: the contract's own notice terms and any 51A period (two months for a contract entered into before 28 April 2026) may still apply, and 51C(c) removes only the 51B(1) notice requirement and the protections that depend on it.
   - Where 51C(c) does not apply, the readings diverge and the firm must decide with legal advice and record why. Give reasons that do not reveal a report or an investigation and that are as detailed and specific as the facts allow. A conservative recommendation, not the statutory test, is never to tell the customer about the suspicion or the disclosure. Under reading A, 51B(4) displaces the 51B(1) to (3) reasons duty to the extent of the conflict, so reasons may be limited to that extent. Under reading B, the 51B(2)(a) duty to give reasons "sufficiently detailed and specific" stands, and no displacement is available. If the only true reason is the suspicion itself, a reasons statement detailed about it risks the offence, and one that withholds it risks 51B(2)(a) non-compliance under reading B.
   - NCA consultation applies only where a disclosure has been made and an NCA officer or unit is engaged. It is neither approval nor immunity, it cannot waive reg 51B, and it must never involve telling the customer about the suspicion. The exceptions read in full (source pack D1) include disclosure to the supervisory authority and for the detection, investigation or prosecution of an offence (POCA s333D(1); TA s21G(1)).

4. **Framework contract question.** Scope: an exit from a correspondent relationship. It excludes cases where reg 31(1)(c) already mandates termination and 51C(a) applies.
   - Reading X (the arrangement is a payment services framework contract) requires, for an indefinite-term contract entered into on or after 28 April 2026 where PSRs Part 6 applies (subject to any reg 40(7) agreement): notice with reasons "sufficiently detailed and specific" (51B(2)(a)); provided as reg 55(1) requires, so on paper or another durable medium, in easily understandable language, and in English or the agreed language (51B(2)(b)); advising how to complain to the firm and of any right to complain to the ombudsman scheme (51B(2)(c)(i) and (ii)); and at least 90 days before termination takes effect (51B(3)). For an indefinite-term contract entered into before 28 April 2026, 51A allows termination by at least two months' notice "if the contract so provides".
   - Reading Y requires: the contract's own termination terms and the general law govern. Under both readings, reg 31(1)(c), the tipping-off provisions (POCA s333A, TA s21D) and the firm's other AML duties apply.
   - Both readings agree: check the contract for a reg 40(7) agreement and its termination clause, and give written notice at least equal to the contractual period and any 51A or 51B period that applies, with reasons where lawful (FinCrimeRadar recommendation). That standard is compatible with either reading.
   - Where the readings diverge, the firm must decide with legal advice and record why: whether a shorter period is permitted. For an urgent exit where no 51C or 51D ground applies, no immediate termination action holds under every reading. A compliant 90-day notice remains available if 51B applies (and the 51A two months if 51A applies). Resolve the contract question (the contract terms and the Schedule 1 para 2(m) analysis) and take legal advice before choosing the timing.

5. **Cure timing.** Reg 31(1) has no cure or grace period. It applies where a relevant person "is unable" to apply the measures as reg 28 requires, and 31(1)(c) says the person "must terminate any existing business relationship". The exceptions in 31(3) to (5) (independent legal professionals and other professional advisers ascertaining a client's legal position or defending a client, the definition of other professional adviser, and insolvency practitioners on stated conditions), and the 31(2) repayment proviso, do not supply a cure period either.
   - Reading 1 requires: evidence supplied before a prohibition or termination duty has crystallised removes the trigger prospectively. Reading 2 requires: a duty that has already accrued persists, and the text does not say what happens to it. Under reading 2, legal advice does not suspend the termination duty.
   - Both readings agree that reg 31(1) contains no grace period and that the exceptions in 31(3) to (5), and the 31(2) proviso, do not supply one.
   - Holds under every reading (reading-neutral steps): fix a short, documented, risk-based deadline for each CDD request, in writing and dated, and keep a dated escalation record.
   - Recommended, not statutory: record the date on which the firm concludes that it is unable to apply the measures, but do not treat that record as the statutory trigger. Reg 31(1) says the firm "is unable", not that it "concludes" it is unable, so a firm may be unable earlier than it records. Apply the reg 31(1)(a) and (b) restrictions from the earlier of the recorded conclusion and the date on which the inability was in fact apparent. Treat late evidence as a fresh reassessment at onboarding standard before any reinstatement. Reg 31 does not provide for that reassessment.
   - Where the readings diverge, the firm must decide with legal advice and record why: whether the firm that has not yet terminated may continue when evidence arrives. No single action holds under every reading in that case.

## 5. Risks to manage in the build

- Do not hard-code the FATF list contents as current. Date-stamp them.
- The reason for narrowing 33(1)(b) may be stated only as HM Treasury's Explanatory Memorandum objective (para 5.2), labelled as such, never as the effect of the regulation.
- Do not attribute the FCTR 12 chapter-level date (01/11/2024) to FCTR 12.3.6G to 12.3.8G. Cite the paragraphs by number and use their own date (13/12/2018) if a date is given (source pack C2).
- Keep the Consumer Duty as an overlay only. Its application turns on retail market business and the distribution-chain rules (PRIN 3.2.7R), and PRIN 2A stays out of scenario verdicts. Quotations from sources in a published guide must be under fifteen words.
- Section 10 discipline for every scenario feedback string: Source, Application, Recommendation kept visibly separate, especially for reg 31 versus reg 34 and 51B versus 51C.
- Section 7 external review is required before publication for the claims listed in the source pack, part H.
