## Page title

Digital Identity Is Not the Whole of CDD: What Verification Does and Does Not Prove

## Standfirst

A certified result can establish one identity proposition. It does not decide who owns a company, why the relationship exists, whether the customer is low risk or whether the evidence remains current.

## Intelligence status

**Evidence checked:** 6 October 2026. Initial source-led draft completed 5 October 2026; regulatory and temporal claims rechecked after independent review on 6 October 2026.

**Current assessment:** Established for the narrow identity-verification proposition described in the February 2026 government guidance. A certified and registered service may support verification of an individual customer's identity, and may also be used for a company director. The regulated firm retains the wider CDD duties and remains responsible for the conclusion.[1][2]

**Status of this brief:** The statutory and government-source boundary is established. The Proof Boundary Record and the five control patterns are FinCrimeRadar assessments. Statements about unregistered services, additional attributes, directors and the supervisory significance of approved guidance remain qualified and require independent review before publication.

**Next trigger:** A change to the February guidance, relevant approved sector guidance, the statutory register, the trust framework, the certification scheme or Regulations 28, 40 or 76.

## How to read this brief

This brief separates three things that can look identical in an onboarding system:

1. **The source says:** What legislation, approved guidance, the statutory register or regulator material actually establishes.
2. **FinCrimeRadar applies:** What that evidence means for the facts in front of the practitioner.
3. **The firm acts:** What should be recorded, resolved or escalated without pretending the source decided more than it did.

The core rule is simple: **a pass is not a customer verdict. It is a bounded evidence result.**

## Executive assessment

The United Kingdom's digital verification regime now gives regulated firms a clearer route for using certified services in identity verification. The February 2026 guidance says a certified service on the statutory register can be treated as a reliable and independent source with anti-impersonation assurance. For an individual customer, the result may be used for the identity-verification limb of Regulation 28. The guidance also permits use for company directors.[1]

That is valuable, but narrow. Regulation 28 still separates identification, verification, purpose and intended nature. It contains further duties for corporate customers, beneficial owners, persons acting for customers, risk-sensitive measures and ongoing monitoring.[2] The government guidance also says digital identity does not fulfil all CDD and does not transfer responsibility away from the regulated firm.[1]

The practical failure is rarely that the result is useless. It is that a useful result is allowed to prove too much.

A green tick can spread across the file until it silently stands for low risk, legitimate activity, valid authority, corporate ownership, clean screening and complete CDD. None of those conclusions follows merely because the identity proposition passed.

The control response is the Proof Boundary Record. It forces the firm to identify the person checked, the proposition established, the exact service scope, the assurance returned, the duties still open and the event that requires reassessment.

## Intelligence timeline

### 26 February 2026: approved MLR guidance

HM Treasury and DSIT published approved guidance for the purposes of MLR compliance. It set the central boundary: certified and registered services can support identity verification, but wider CDD remains with the firm. Approved status gives the guidance relevance under Regulation 76; it is not a safe harbour.[1][4]

### June 2026: trust framework 1.0 finalised

The trust framework moved to version 1.0. It defines service roles, rules and identity outcomes for natural persons and attributes, while keeping the certified service, not a relying firm's whole onboarding process, as the relevant object.[7]

### 20 August 2026: identity and attributes clarified

OfDIA clarified that the firm selects a confidence level commensurate with risk and that an identity outcome is different from additional attributes such as address information, PEP checks or sanctions screening.[8]

### 2 September 2026: certification against 1.0 becomes live

The certification scheme table records scheme 1.0.1 as live from 2 September 2026. It also records Gamma scheme 0.4.4 as live from 1 July 2025 with an expiry date of 1 December 2028. The transition is a reason to record the version used, not a reason to assume one service is suitable or unsuitable.[7]

## What changed, and what did not

The new guidance reduces one area of uncertainty. A firm does not have to treat every certified digital identity result as merely an informal lead. Within the stated scope, a certified and registered service can provide the reliable and independent identity evidence contemplated by Regulation 28.[1][2]

What did not change is the structure of CDD. The firm must still understand which subject was checked and which proposition was established. It must still address purpose, risk, corporate facts, authority, ownership, beneficial ownership, enhanced measures and ongoing monitoring where those duties apply.[1][2]

The statutory register reinforces the need for precision. It records named services and exposes information including role type, trust-framework version, certificate dates, identity profiles and supplementary codes. A provider may operate services with different roles and scopes.[5][6] The question is therefore not only, “Is the provider registered?” It is, “Was this exact service registered and certified for the role and scope relied on when the check occurred?”

## The proof boundary

An onboarding response can contain several outputs, but they do not necessarily share one evidential status.

An identity outcome answers a question about a natural person's identity at a stated confidence. An address attribute answers a different question. PEP and sanctions screening concern still different propositions. Corporate identity, authority, ownership and control concern different subjects and evidence again.[2][8]

The boundary matters because certification attaches to specified services and roles. A firm using a certified identity component must not represent its whole onboarding process as certified.[7] Equally, a successful director check is evidence about that director. It is not automatically evidence of the company's identity, the director's authority, the ownership structure or the beneficial owner.[1][2]

### Risk, Signal, Response: The Green Tick Halo

**Metaphor:** One successful check lights up the whole customer file.

**Risk:** A valid identity outcome is treated as evidence that the relationship is low risk and CDD is complete.

**Signal:** The case status changes to complete when the vendor returns a pass, although purpose, risk or enhanced measures remain unresolved.

**Response:** Write the Proof Boundary Record, then make a separate decision on wider CDD completeness.

## The six-field Proof Boundary Record

The Proof Boundary Record is a FinCrimeRadar control model. It does not replace the legislation or the firm's risk assessment. It prevents a result from silently acquiring meanings that its source and scope do not support.

### 1. Subject

Record exactly who was checked. “Customer” is too broad where the customer is a company and the checked person is a director or representative. Use the natural person and their role: individual customer, director, representative or beneficial owner.

### 2. Proposition

State what the result establishes. In the central use case, the proposition is the subject's identity at a stated level of confidence. Do not write “customer verified” if the file could read that as proof of low risk, legitimate activity, authority or complete CDD.

### 3. Service scope

Record the named registered service, certified role, framework version, certificate status and any relevant identity profile or supplementary code that applied at the time of the check.[5][6][7]

### 4. Assurance

Record the confidence level or profile returned and why it was commensurate with the assessed risk. The firm, not the vendor brand, remains responsible for choosing an appropriate level.[2][8]

### 5. Residual duties

List what remains open. Depending on the customer and facts, this may include purpose and intended nature, authority, corporate identity, ownership and control, beneficial ownership, EDD, ongoing monitoring and record keeping.[1][2][3]

### 6. Change trigger

State what makes the boundary stale. Triggers can include a change in service registration or certificate scope, a higher risk assessment, new behaviour inconsistent with the stated purpose, a change in ownership or authority, or new guidance.

### Risk, Signal, Response: The Brand-Level Shortcut

**Metaphor:** The provider's name replaces the service evidence.

**Risk:** Procurement or operations confirms a familiar provider but not the exact registered service, certified role or certificate that produced the result.

**Signal:** The file records only the vendor name or a generic statement that the provider is certified.

**Response:** Record and check the exact service, role, framework version, certificate status and relevant scope at the time of use.

### Risk, Signal, Response: Attribute Spillover

**Metaphor:** Every field in one response inherits the status of the identity result.

**Risk:** Address, PEP or sanctions outputs are assumed to be covered by identity-service certification because they appear in the same response.

**Signal:** The control record cannot map each output to a certified role, stated scope and source.

**Response:** Separate identity outcomes from attributes and screening, then evidence the status and scope of each output independently.

## Settled and unresolved

### Settled enough to use

- A certified and registered service may support the individual identity-verification proposition described in the February guidance.[1]
- That result does not complete every CDD duty.[1][2]
- The exact service, certified role and assurance matter more than the provider brand.[5][6][7]
- The firm selects assurance appropriate to risk and remains responsible for the CDD conclusion.[1][2][8]
- Identity, attributes, screening and wider customer-risk conclusions are separate propositions.[2][8]

### Keep qualified

- The government guidance says a service that is not certified and on the register cannot reliably be deemed suitable under that guidance. That is narrower than saying every use of unregistered electronic evidence is prohibited by statute.[1]
- A director result does not by itself prove the corporate customer, authority, ownership, control or beneficial ownership.[1][2]
- Attribute outputs may sit inside or outside certified scope depending on the service and role.[6][8]
- Approved guidance has statutory relevance under Regulation 76, but this brief does not describe it as a safe harbour.[1][4]
- Register status, certificate scope, framework version and service configuration can change.[5][6][7]

## Decision horizon

### Now

Inventory every digital verification service and output used in onboarding. Match it to the exact register entry, role, certificate and framework version. Find cases where a pass currently closes purpose, risk, screening or corporate checks without separate evidence.

### Next control cycle

Add the six fields to policy, procurement, onboarding configuration and quality assurance. Define assurance by risk. Test whether reviewers can reconstruct what the result proved and why the wider file was considered complete or incomplete. The FCA's 2026 CDD review said most reviewed firms had documented identity-verification procedures, but few gave staff enough practical detail. It separately said several firms distinguished standard CDD from EDD and most tailored CDD to customer risk. The FCA presented clear differentiation and risk-tailored CDD as examples of good practice.[9]

### On change

Reassess when the service or certificate changes, when assurance no longer matches the risk, when customer facts or behaviour change, or when new guidance changes the boundary.

## Operational implementation

### For policy owners

Replace generic statements such as “use an approved provider” with the exact conditions that make a result usable: subject, service, role, scope, assurance and date. State explicitly that the result closes only the recorded proposition.

### For procurement and assurance

Evaluate the service that will be used, not only the provider. Preserve the register and certificate facts that supported the decision. Define how a scope, version or certificate change reaches the control owner.

### For onboarding and systems teams

Keep identity, attributes, screening, corporate facts, authority and risk as separate decision fields. A vendor response may populate more than one field, but each output needs its own source and status. Do not let one response code close the entire case.

### For reviewers and MLRO teams

Ask whether the recorded proposition matches the evidence. Then ask what remains open. A correct identity result should usually be retained even when a different part of the relationship is inconsistent. The correct response is to investigate the inconsistency, not to erase useful evidence.

### For records management

Retain enough information to reconstruct both the evidence and the firm's decision. Regulation 40 requires retention of CDD material and supporting transaction records within its statutory conditions. A vendor pass alone may not explain the service scope, assurance selected or residual duties considered.[3]

### Risk, Signal, Response: The Frozen Pass

**Metaphor:** A dated result is treated as permanently current.

**Risk:** The firm retains the original pass but does not define what would make its evidential boundary stale.

**Signal:** The record has no service, certificate, customer, risk or behavioural change trigger.

**Response:** Add a change trigger and reassess when the recorded service scope, customer facts or risk context changes.

## Worked decision 1: the verified customer with an unexplained relationship

An individual customer returns a valid result from a certified and registered service at an assurance level selected for the initial risk. The declared business purpose is incomplete and the first funding pattern is inconsistent with the expected activity.

**What should the analyst do?**

**A. Complete CDD because identity passed.** High-risk shortcut. It allows evidence about identity to decide purpose and relationship risk.

**B. Reject the identity result and restart verification.** Incomplete approach. The wider inconsistency does not, without more, make the identity result false.

**C. Accept the identity proposition, keep purpose and risk open, and record the boundary.** Strongest option.

### Source, Application and Action reasoning

**Source:** The February guidance confines the relevant benefit to identity verification and says wider CDD remains with the firm. Regulation 28 separately addresses purpose and intended nature, risk-sensitive measures and ongoing monitoring.[1][2]

**Application:** The identity evidence has not been displaced by the funding inconsistency. The inconsistency affects another proposition: whether the stated purpose and risk assessment remain credible.

**Action:** Preserve the identity result. Record its subject, service scope and assurance. Resolve the purpose and funding inconsistency, reassess risk and apply EDD if required. Do not mark the wider file complete while those tasks remain open.

**Counterfactual:** If coherent purpose evidence is obtained and subsequent activity matches it, the relationship proposition may close. The original identity result does not acquire a wider scope.

## Worked decision 2: the verified director of an unresolved company

A director completes a registered digital identity check. The customer is a company, signing authority is unclear, ownership information is layered and a beneficial owner has not been independently established.

**What can the analyst conclude?**

**A. The company and beneficial owner are verified because the director passed.** High-risk shortcut. It moves evidence across subjects and propositions.

**B. The director result is irrelevant because the customer is a company.** Incomplete approach. Valid evidence about the director still belongs in the file.

**C. The director's identity is evidenced, while corporate identity, authority, ownership, control and beneficial ownership remain separate tasks.** Strongest option.

### Source, Application and Action reasoning

**Source:** The February guidance permits use for company directors. Regulation 28 separately addresses corporate particulars, ownership and control, beneficial owners and a person's authority to act.[1][2]

**Application:** The result is relevant and bounded. It concerns one natural person. It does not establish every fact about the company or the person's relationship to it.

**Action:** Retain the result in the director's subject record. Keep corporate identity, authority, ownership, control and beneficial ownership open until separately evidenced.

**Counterfactual:** If those corporate and authority propositions are independently established, their fields may close because of that evidence, not because the director result changed scope.

### Risk, Signal, Response: The Verified Director Mirage

**Metaphor:** Proof about one person is made to stand in for the company.

**Risk:** A director's successful identity check is treated as proof of the corporate customer, the director's authority, ownership, control or beneficial ownership.

**Signal:** One result is copied across several subjects or propositions in the CDD record.

**Response:** Keep the director's identity result, then verify the corporate customer, authority, ownership, control and beneficial owners as separate propositions.

## Knowledge check

### Question 1

A provider appears on the statutory DVS register. What must the firm check next?

A. Nothing, because provider registration covers every service and output.

B. The exact service, role, certificate, framework version and relevant scope used.

C. Only whether the provider's contract says it supports AML.

**Correct answer:** B. Registration and certification are service-specific.[5][6][7]

### Question 2

A certified identity response includes an address and sanctions result. What follows?

A. Every output is certified because it appears in one response.

B. The identity result must be discarded because screening is separate.

C. Each output must be mapped to its own role, scope and source before its status is stated.

**Correct answer:** C. Identity, attributes and screening are separate propositions.[8]

### Question 3

A company director passes the identity check. Which conclusion is strongest?

A. The director's identity may be evidenced, while company identity, authority, ownership, control and beneficial ownership remain separate.

B. The company and all beneficial owners are verified.

C. The result has no relevance because the customer is not a natural person.

**Correct answer:** A. The result is useful for one subject and does not complete the company file.[1][2]

### Question 4

The customer identity result is valid but the stated purpose conflicts with early account activity. What should happen?

A. The identity result proves the activity is legitimate.

B. Keep the identity evidence and investigate the separate purpose and risk inconsistency.

C. Delete the identity result and start the same check again.

**Correct answer:** B. The propositions must not be collapsed.[1][2]

### Question 5

Which record best supports later review?

A. “Provider passed customer.”

B. A screenshot of the green tick only.

C. The subject, proposition, service scope, assurance, residual duties and change trigger, with the underlying evidence retained under the firm's records framework.

**Correct answer:** C. The Proof Boundary Record preserves the decision boundary and complements the statutory records duty.[3]

## FAQ

### Is every service from a registered provider suitable for every AML identity check?

No. Check the exact named service, certified role, current certificate and relevant scope. Suitability also depends on whether the assurance is commensurate with the firm's risk assessment.[5][6][8]

### Does a successful DVS result mean the customer is low risk?

No. It can support an identity proposition. Customer risk, purpose, activity and any enhanced measures require their own evidence and decision.[1][2]

### Can an unregistered electronic verification service never be used?

The February guidance says a service that is not certified and on the register cannot reliably be deemed suitable for MLR identity verification under that guidance.[1] Do not turn that statement into a universal statutory prohibition. A firm considering another route should check the legislation, relevant sector guidance and legal position rather than assuming equivalence.

### Does checking a director complete company CDD?

No. The director result can be valid evidence about that natural person. Corporate identity, authority, ownership, control and beneficial ownership are separate matters under Regulation 28.[1][2]

### Are address, PEP and sanctions outputs part of the certified identity check?

Not automatically. They are separate attributes or screening propositions. Their status depends on the service role, certified scope and source relied on.[6][8]

### What should the firm retain?

Retain the material required by Regulation 40 and enough context to reconstruct why the result was relied on and what remained open.[3] The six-field record should sit alongside, not replace, the underlying evidence and the firm's wider CDD record.
