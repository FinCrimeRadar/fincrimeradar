# The De-Risking Judgement Call: external review brief (CLAUDE.md section 7)

For a manual ChatGPT or Codex review outside Claude Code. No prose has been drafted for the guide, so you are reviewing claims and readings, not copy.

**Instruction to the reviewer.** Test each claim against the primary text, not against our source pack. The pack (`docs/review-queue/de-risking-judgement-call-source-pack.md`) records what we read and what we concluded. Treat its conclusions as hypotheses to break. Fetch the primary provision yourself, quote the operative words, and say where you disagree, including where the pack's quotation is wrong or incomplete. If a provision cannot be retrieved, say so and do not fill the gap from memory.

**Primary text to fetch (current revised versions, as at 21 September 2026).**

- SI 2026/621: regs 1, 19 and 20, and the Explanatory Note printed at the end of the SI (legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/621/made), which says of itself "This note is not part of the Regulations". **Separately**, the HM Treasury Explanatory Memorandum, a different document laid before Parliament for the Joint Committee on Statutory Instruments (legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/621/memorandum/contents, PDF uksiem_20260621_en_001.pdf), paras 5.2, 5.5 and 5.6. Do not treat the Note and the Memorandum as the same document, and say what status each has.
- PSRs 2017 (SI 2017/752): regs 2(1), 40, 51 to 51D and Schedule 1 Part 2 para 2(m), as inserted or substituted by SI 2025/688. PARs 2015 reg 26 as amended.
- FCA Handbook, application rules first: PRIN 2A.1.3G (and 2A.1.4G to 2A.1.7R), PRIN 3.2.6R to PRIN 3.2.12G, the Glossary definitions of "retail market business" and "retail customer", BCOBS 1.1, and FG22/5 chapter 2. Then the substantive text: PRIN 2A.2.8R, PRIN 2A.2.10G(3) and PRIN 2A.2.10G(5)(iv). Also FCTR 12 (chapter level) and FCTR 12.3.6G to 12.3.8G (paragraph level).

Do not rely on the pack's extracts,** and say if the current text differs from what the pack quotes. FATF Guidance on Financial Inclusion and AML/CFT Measures (June 2025), paras 47, 52, 53, 140 to 142.
- JMLSG Part I (Aug 2025) 4.33, 4.57, 4.62, 4.74 to 4.77, 5.2.6 to 5.2.7. Part II (Dec 2025) 16.1, 16.27, 16.28, 16.32 to 16.34.

**Claims to test.** For each, give a verdict from: Supported / Supported with conditions / Not supported / Cannot determine from the text.

1. **Reg 33(1)(b) and the lists.** What did reg 33(3)(a) say before and after 30 June 2026, and what follows for FATF Increased Monitoring jurisdictions as a mandatory EDD trigger? Separately: how, if at all, may the Increased Monitoring list bear on the firm's assessment under reg 33 (for example through 33(6)(c), 33(1)(a), 33(1)(g) or 33(7)), or does the text say something else? Note that the statute never names that list after the amendment. Also: what does the Explanatory Memorandum (paras 5.2, 5.5, 5.6) say about the objective of the change, and what weight may a guide give it given that it is not part of the instrument?
2. **Reg 31 and "customer due diligence measures".** Reg 31(1) says "unable to apply customer due diligence measures as required by regulation 28". Reg 3(1) defines the term as "the measures required by regulation 28, and where relevant, those required by regulations 29 and 33 to 37". Does inability to complete reg 33 EDD or reg 34(1) measures engage reg 31? Set out each reading the text allows and the strongest textual argument for each. Say whether any authority (case, FCA, Treasury) resolves it. Post-drafting additions for this item (source pack section I): reg 3(1) also defines "enhanced customer due diligence measures" as "the customer due diligence measures required under regulations 33 to 35", presented in the guide as one argument for the wide reading beside the "as required by regulation 28" anchor; and 31(1)(b) is now paraphrased as two limbs (establishing a relationship, and a transaction otherwise than through a bank account). Please test both.
3. **Reg 34 scope.** To whom do the 34(1) measures apply, and what does 33(1)(c) ("in accordance with regulation 34") add, if anything, for a respondent that is not from a third country, or for a relationship that does not involve the execution of payments ("correspondent relationship" is defined at reg 34(4) via reg 3(1))?
4. **PSRs 2017 regs 51B and 51C.** Test: the 28 April 2026 vintage condition and reg 51A; the 51C(a) and (c) exemptions; whether a discretionary AML exit (CDD complete, no suspicion) falls inside 51B; how the 51B(2)(a) reasons duty sits with 51B(4) and the tipping-off offences (POCA s333A, TA s21D); and the complete reg 40(7) test: the payment service user is none of a consumer, a micro-enterprise or a charity (with the reg 2(1) definitions of each, including that micro-enterprise status is judged when the contract for payment services is entered into and that "charity" means a body with annual income below £1 million), **and** the parties to the contract have agreed that any or all of the provisions of Part 6 do not apply. Both limbs are needed. State whether an agreement exists in each fact pattern you test. Also whether a correspondent banking arrangement is a payment services framework contract at all.
5. **PRIN 2A and AML exits.** First test the application rule, not only the substantive text: PRIN 2A.1.3G, PRIN 3.2.6R to 3.2.12G (especially 3.2.8R, 3.2.10R and 3.2.11G), the Glossary definitions of "retail market business" and "retail customer" (limb (2) is activity-based), BCOBS 1.1, and FG22/5 chapter 2. Then ask whether PRIN 2A.2.8R, PRIN 2A.2.10G(3) or PRIN 2A.2.10G(5)(iv) applies to an AML-driven account closure. State what the text says, what it does not say, and whether any FCA statement answers the point. We treat any such application as our own assessment.
6. **JMLSG 16.27.** It says correspondent banking firms "must apply the Enhanced Due Diligence measures in full". Compare with reg 34(1), 34(4) and 33(1)(c). Is the JMLSG "must" broader than the statute?
7. **FATF Guidance para 142.** It says regulated entities are "only required under FATF Standards to terminate or reject customer relationships, on a case-by-case basis, where the ML/TF risks cannot be mitigated". Check this against the Standards themselves (R.10, R.13, INR.10), not only the Guidance. Is it a fair statement of the Standards, and is any UK legal consequence being wrongly implied when read next to reg 31?
8. **Scenario facts and outcome labels (section 10).** The scenarios below state facts. They do not state our conclusions. For each variant, tell us: which provision drives the outcome, whether the stated facts are legally sufficient to reach it, which further fact would be needed if not, and whether describing the outcome as mandatory or discretionary is supported by the primary text, or is inference presented as law. Outcome labels we use: ordinary continuation; enhanced continuation; conditional or restricted continuation; discretionary decline or exit; mandatory decline or termination. If a variant's facts do not settle the label, say "cannot determine" and name the missing fact.

   **Scenario A, correspondent relationship.** Fixed facts to test for sufficiency: the firm is a UK credit institution; the respondent is a credit institution or financial institution established outside the UK; the relationship involves the execution of payments; the respondent is not a shell bank and is not known to allow its accounts to be used by a shell bank; there is no sanctions designation or other legal prohibition on the relationship; the respondent's jurisdiction status is stated with its FATF list date (as at 19 June 2026: Increased Monitoring, Call for Action, or neither); and the firm has completed reg 28 CDD on the respondent as its customer, with the answers to any reg 34(1)(a) to (f) requests stated in each variant.
   - A1: jurisdiction on Increased Monitoring only. The respondent answers some of the reg 34(1)(a) to (c) requests and not others.
   - A2: as A1, but the jurisdiction is on the Call for Action list in its enhanced due diligence tier (as at 19 June 2026, Myanmar), stated with the date.
   - A3: as A1, but the respondent declines to confirm that it has verified and monitors the customers who have direct access to the firm's accounts (reg 34(1)(f)).
   - A4: as A1, but the respondent is a shell bank, or is known to allow its accounts to be used by one.
   - A5: as A1, but the respondent is established in an EEA state that is on neither FATF list.
   - A6: as A1, but the correspondent relationship does not involve the execution of payments (for example a securities settlement arrangement only).
   - A7: as A1, but the respondent is established in the United Kingdom.

   **Scenario B, UK customer.** Fixed facts to test for sufficiency: state (i) whether the firm is unable to apply reg 28 CDD, and if so which measure (identifying or verifying the customer, identifying or verifying a beneficial owner, the purpose and intended nature of the relationship, or ongoing monitoring under reg 28(11)); (ii) whether the account is under an indefinite-term framework contract for payment services; (iii) the contract's entry date relative to 28 April 2026; (iv) the customer's classification for PSRs Part 6 (consumer, micro-enterprise at the entry date, charity with income below £1 million, or none of these); (v) whether the parties have a reg 40(7) agreement and what it disapplies; (vi) whether the firm has reasonable grounds to suspect serious crime or has made a disclosure under POCA or the Terrorism Act; and (vii) whether any information the firm seeks is EDD information beyond the reg 28 measures.
   - B1: the account is under an indefinite-term framework contract for payment services entered into before 28 April 2026; the firm is unable to identify and verify a beneficial owner under reg 28(4) after trying alternatives; the customer is a micro-enterprise (judged at the entry date); no suspicion; no reg 40(7) agreement.
   - B2: as B1, but the contract was entered into on or after 28 April 2026.
   - B3: as B2, but the customer is a body corporate that is not a consumer, not a micro-enterprise and not a charity (a charity being a body with annual income below £1 million), and the parties have agreed under reg 40(7) that Part 6 does not apply.
   - B4: the account is under an indefinite-term framework contract for payment services entered into on or after 28 April 2026; reg 28 CDD is fully applied; residual risk stays high after mitigants; no suspicion and no disclosure made; the customer is a charity (annual income below £1 million); no reg 40(7) agreement.
   - B5: as B4, but the customer has not supplied source of funds information the firm asked for as EDD, while every reg 28 measure has been applied.
   - B6: as B4, but the firm has made a disclosure under POCA or the Terrorism Act and has reasonable grounds to suspect that a payment service provided under the framework contract has been used, is being used or will be used in connection with serious crime.
   - B7: as B4, but the customer is an individual who is a consumer, acting for purposes outside any trade, business or profession.
